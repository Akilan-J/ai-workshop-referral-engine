How AI was used, and where its output was changed

I built this with Claude as a coding and planning partner. Below are three decisions where the first AI output was not the final one. Each follows the same format: prompt, AI output, what was changed or rejected, and the final result.

Working asset ↗ Growth plan (PDF) ↗ Code ↗

01The funnel math

  1. Prompt
    [Challenge PDF] Plan how to get 500 final-year students to register in 7 days on ₹2,000.
  2. AI output

    A referral system instead of the landing page everyone else would build. The funnel math was one channel: 40 WhatsApp groups × 250 members × 5% ≈ 500.

  3. Changed

    I kept the referral idea and rejected the math:

    • It needed 40 groups to all convert at 5%, with no buffer.
    • It ignored the referral loop the asset was built for.
    • It had nothing to say about what to do if Day 2 falls behind.
  4. Final

    Two seed channels plus a loop:

    • 25 student champions × 240 × 4% = 240
    • 12 club and placement-cell partners × 250 × 3% = 90
    • A referral loop at k ≈ 0.37, adding 190

    Total 520, a 4% buffer. The plan includes a sensitivity check: at k = 0.2 the total is about 410, so recruit 8 more champions. The dashboard tracks pace against this exact daily plan.

02Where the asset runs

  1. Prompt
    Start building it.
  2. AI output

    The fastest route was to ship it as a Claude Artifact: hosted in one step, with no deployment.

  3. Rejected

    Opening an Artifact requires a claude.ai account, and students don't have one. A growth asset with a login wall loses most visitors before the form.

  4. Final

    A static site on GitHub Pages with a Google Sheet as the database, reached through an Apps Script API. It's free, needs no login, and the team can read registrations in a normal spreadsheet.

    One logic file runs in the browser, on the backend and in 12 automated tests.

03Demo vs real data

  1. Prompt
    Is there a centralised DB?
  2. AI output

    Not yet. The live site was running in demo mode: about 240 made-up registrations created in each visitor's own browser. Two students on two phones would never see each other.

  3. Changed

    The site looked complete, but questioning it showed it couldn't run a real campaign as deployed. Demo data is right for reviewers, who can try every feature without polluting real numbers. It's wrong for launch day.

  4. Final

    Demo mode stays on the public reviewer link. The Google Sheet backend is ready and tested against a simulated Sheet. Going live means setting one line in config.js (API_URL), and that is in the plan's prep day.

What AI suggested that I deliberately rejected

The first demo leaderboard. The AI generated sample data where one ambassador had 25 referrals and everyone else had a handful.

I rejected it because a leaderboard nobody can catch kills the referral race it's supposed to drive. A student who sees #1 at 25 and themselves at 0 doesn't start sharing. It also looked fake to a reviewer.

The data was rebalanced so #1 is around 16 and the reward milestones (3, 5, 10) look reachable. That is the experience real students need on Day 1.
